07 · Tracking seizures

The record your vet needs

Treatment decisions depend on how often seizures happen and what pattern they follow, not on memory. A consistent log turns "he has been having more lately" into a trend your vet can use.

What to log every time

date and time—
durationseconds or minutes
typefocal or generalized
trigger (if any)stress, fatigue, etc.
warning signs beforepacing, hiding, clinging
what happened duringsee below
recovery time afterhow long to act normal
video capturedyes or no
rescue medication givenwhich and what time

Warning signs before a seizure (called pre-ictal signs in medical terms) are changes that come before the seizure, such as restlessness, pacing, hiding, or clinging to you. These are worth noting even if the seizure never happens.

What happens during the seizure (called ictal phase signs) describes the event itself. For a generalized seizure: stiffening, jerking, drooling, or loss of bladder control. For a focal seizure: twitching, snapping at the air, or an unusual behavior.

Emergencies: Clusters (two or more seizures within 24 hours) and any seizure lasting more than 5 minutes need immediate vet or emergency clinic care. Do not finish the log first.

Tracking apps

R

RVC Pet Epilepsy Tracker

Free app from the Royal Veterinary College. Seizure and medication logs, reminders, and exports you can email to your vet.

S

Safetail

Seizure logs with video upload and a shared dashboard your vet can follow between visits.

M

MediPaws

A broader pet health tracker with a seizure section: timelines, medication reminders, and PDF reports for your vet.

P

Pet Vitality

A pet health app that includes seizure and medication tracking alongside other health records. Currently being tried out as an option.

07a · Notes and video

Video and notes

Video

Why a video matters

A short clip of a seizure is worth more to your vet than any written description. It shows how the seizure starts, what it looks like, how long it lasts, and how your dog recovers.

What to capture: Keep the phone steady enough to see your dog's body. Thirty to sixty seconds is usually enough. Try to catch the start, but the middle or end is still useful.

Notes

Notes section

Use notes for anything else that might matter: how your dog seemed before, unusual activity or stress that day, medication timing, diet changes, or anything else you noticed. Over time these notes help you and your vet spot patterns.

Examples: "Rough night, did not sleep well." "Started a new treat yesterday." "Stressful grooming visit that morning." "Seemed anxious before this one." "Happened about 2 hours after the morning dose."
